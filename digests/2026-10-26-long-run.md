# Long run digest, 2026-10-26

Not legal advice. Legal items are a briefing for a lawyer. Review verdicts come from `findings/review.md`; many sources were secondary or blocked.

## What was researched
101 department findings (legal 43, technical 27, market 31) plus 17 red-team attacks (R-001..R-017), 10 kill criteria, an outreach draft pack (not sent), and a synthesis. Two research passes, two review passes.

## Strongest verified findings
- **Legal:** India has no trade-secret statute; protection rests on contract and confidence (L-001). US DTSA needs reasonable secrecy measures and treats reverse engineering and independent derivation as lawful (L-030, L-031). DPDP core duties and cross-border rules start about 13 May 2027 (L-008, L-009). Indian Supreme Court (2020) held algorithmic pricing is not inherently collusion (L-033); US agencies withdrew competitor-collaboration guidelines in Dec 2024 (L-038). GDPR Recital 26 tests "means reasonably likely" to identify (L-040).
- **Technical:** Agent-based attacks extract over 70% of a private KB via benign-looking queries (T-004, T-005). Query-stream anomaly detection works in tests but has no adaptive-attacker evaluation (T-027). DP for free-text answers is possible via synthetic databases (T-025); DP utility is contested (T-026). x402 and Stripe/Metronome give per-call and metered billing (T-016, T-023).
- **Market:** Consortia (Apheris/OpenFold3) already serve pharma data sharing (M-008). Clean-room pricing is per compute-hour, not per answer (M-004).
