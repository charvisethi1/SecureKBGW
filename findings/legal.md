## Findings
- [L-001] India has no dedicated trade secret statute; courts protect secrets via contract, equity (breach of confidence), copyright and sometimes other law, so protection depends on contract and on proving confidentiality rather than on a statutory right | https://www.tradecommissioner.gc.ca/en/market-industry-info/search-country-region/country/canada-india-export/trade-secret-protection.html | med | India | 2026-10-26 | status: unreviewed
- [L-002] Indian breach-of-confidence doctrine (per commentary) needs three elements: quality of confidence, information shared in circumstances imposing an obligation of confidence, and unauthorised use; no written contract strictly required. Implication (analyst inference): the gateway must impose express confidentiality and use limits on buyers, otherwise answers may be treated as unrestricted disclosure | https://www.tradecommissioner.gc.ca/en/market-industry-info/search-country-region/country/canada-india-export/trade-secret-protection.html | low | India | 2026-10-26 | status: unreviewed
- [L-003] Section 27 of the Indian Contract Act voids agreements in restraint of trade; a broad buyer-side restriction on using answers could be challenged as a disguised restraint, while clauses that specifically identify protected information stand on firmer ground. Calcutta High Court (2022) enforced confidentiality against ex-employees yet called for a re-look at s.27 | https://www.livelaw.in/amp/news-updates/calcutta-high-court-trade-secrets-section-27-indian-contract-act-restraint-clause-196985 | med | India | 2026-10-26 | status: unreviewed
- [L-004] EU: Directive 2016/943 Art. 2(1) protects information only if it is secret, has commercial value because secret, and has been subject to reasonable steps by its lawful controller to keep it secret; so a seller that sells answers without strong access controls and use restrictions risks losing protected status. Art. 3 treats independent discovery and lawful observation or testing as lawful acquisition (reverse-engineering risk if answers accumulate) | https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eudr/2016/943/article/2 | med | EU | 2026-10-26 | status: unreviewed
- [L-005] OBSTACLE / disconfirming: answering many queries can let a buyer reconstruct the underlying data; in both India (confidence quality) and EU (reasonable steps, Art. 3 lawful derivation) this could erode trade-secret status or make the buyer's derivation lawful. This legal risk is analyst inference from L-001 and L-004, not a stated holding; no case on query-answer leakage was found | https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eudr/2016/943/article/2 | low | EU | 2026-10-26 | status: unreviewed
- [L-006] Ownership of unused experiment data in India is not settled by a single statute: raw facts are generally not copyright-protected and any ownership claim rests on contract, confidence and employee/third-party assignments (collaborators, CROs, funders). Not verified from a primary source this cycle; the finding is a gap, not a conclusion | https://www.tradecommissioner.gc.ca/en/market-industry-info/search-country-region/country/canada-india-export/trade-secret-protection.html | low | India | 2026-10-26 | status: unreviewed
- [L-007] Licence vs disclosure vs service characterisation is not answered by any primary source found this cycle. - [L-008] DPDP Rules 2025 were notified 13-14 November 2025; Rules 1, 2 and 17-21 took effect at once, and most operative rules (3, 5-16, 22, 23) commence 18 months later, around 13 May 2027, so the core duties are not yet in force as of 2026-10-26 | https://www.hoganlovells.com/en/publications/indias-digital-personal-data-protection-act-2023-brought-into-force- | med | India | 2026-10-26 | status: unreviewed
- [L-009] DPDP cross-border transfer (s.16, Rule 15) has not commenced (expected 13 May 2027); India uses a restrict-by-notification model (government may bar transfers to listed countries) not a whitelist, and existing sectoral localisation rules still apply meanwhile | https://ksandk.com/data-protection-and-data-privacy/cross-border-data-transfers-dpdp-act-india/ | med | India | 2026-10-26 | status: unreviewed
- [L-010] DPDP Schedule caps: up to Rs 250 crore for failing reasonable security safeguards (s.8(5)), Rs 200 crore for breach-notification failures and children's data, Rs 150 crore for Significant Data Fiduciary duties, Rs 50 crore for other breaches. A platform whose answers carry personal data could be a data fiduciary or processor with exposure on these heads. - [L-011] DPDP s.3 (per secondary summary) excludes personal data made publicly available by the data principal, but covers digital personal data processed in India and processing abroad linked to offering goods or services to people in India. It has no general B2B exemption, so seller-supplied experiment data containing personal data (trial subjects, researchers) is in scope. Section numbering not checked against official text | https://dpdpa.com/theschedule.html | low | India | 2026-10-26 | status: unreviewed
- [L-012] GDPR Art. 4(1) and Recital 26: data is personal if a person is identifiable by means reasonably likely to be used by the controller or anyone else; truly anonymous data is outside GDPR but pseudonymised data stays personal. Query answers derived from data on identifiable people (for example clinical or lab-staff data) may stay in scope, and EDPB anonymisation guidance is still draft per commentary | https://verasafe.com/blog/anonymous-for-whom-key-takeaways-from-the-edpbs-draft-anonymization-guidelines/ | low | EU | 2026-10-26 | status: unreviewed

- [L-017] India Competition Act: the 2023 amendment added a s.3(3) proviso presuming a non-competing hub (such as a platform) is party to a horizontal agreement if it participates or intends to participate; "intention" is untested. - [L-018] India: in Samir Agrawal v CCI (Ola/Uber) hub-and-spoke allegations failed for lack of evidence of an agreement among the competing drivers, so a platform is not automatically liable; the question turns on evidence of horizontal coordination. ## Questions for a lawyer
1. Under Indian law, who owns unused experiment data when collaborators, CROs, employees or funders contributed, and can the seller licence it freely? Is s.19 assignment scope (five years, India only by default) a problem? (L-001, L-006, L-015, L-016, L-037, L-042, L-043)
2. Does selling a query answer risk loss of confidential status or reasonable-steps protection, especially through aggregation or reconstruction by many queries? What contract and technical controls would courts accept? (L-002, L-004, L-005, L-030 to L-032)
3. Is a restriction on buyer use of answers enforceable given Contract Act s.27? (L-003)
4. How should answers be characterised (limited licence, service or disclosure), and what follows for IP, tax and liability? (L-007)
5. Which DPDP duties apply before and after the May 2027 commencement, and who is data fiduciary versus processor among seller, platform and buyer? (L-008, L-010, L-011, L-034, L-035)
6. When is an answer anonymous enough to fall outside DPDP and GDPR, and what testing standard is defensible? (L-012, L-013, L-040)
7. What cross-border rules apply to a seller, platform and buyer in different countries, now and after s.16 commences? (L-009)
8. EU/US data-transfer mechanisms for this model, and US state UTSA differences from DTSA. (L-014, L-030 to L-032)
9. Would a pay-per-query gateway between competing firms be a "hub" under Competition Act s.3(3) proviso, EU Art. 101 or Sherman s.1, and what vetting or aggregation would make it defensible? Does Samir Agrawal still help after the 2023 amendment? (L-017 to L-021, L-033, L-038)
10. Can the platform rely on IT Act s.79 at all given it composes and prices AI answers, or should it assume direct liability? What is the current Amway/Louboutin position and does the Feb 2026 IT Rules amendment matter? (L-022, L-023, L-028, L-039)
11. Does Indian law impose negligent-misstatement liability for wrong answers, and will a cap or disclaimer hold when an AI agent acts on the answer? (L-025, L-026, L-036)
12. What platform-seller-buyer contract structure (accuracy warranties, indemnities, back-to-back caps, EU Data Act fairness) do you recommend? (L-027, L-029)
13. Is a "data broker" regime relevant in India, EU or US states for this platform? (L-024, L-041)
